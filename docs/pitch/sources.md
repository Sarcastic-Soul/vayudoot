# Sources for the pitch

Every number in the deck, the video script and the description comes from this
file. Each entry gives the figure, the year the data is from, where it was
published, a link, and the exact words it rests on. Use the pitch line as
written; if you need to change the wording, check it against the quote first.

Checked in September 2026. Quotes marked *(read directly)* were read on the page
or PDF itself. The rest were read by a research pass and checked for wording but
not re-opened.

## 1. Deaths from air pollution

### India: more than 2.01 million deaths in 2023

> **Pitch line:** Air pollution killed more than 2 million people in India in
> 2023, nearly one death in five.

- **Figure:** "more than 2.01 million deaths from air pollution"; "nearly 20% of
  all deaths in India in 2023". Covers outdoor PM2.5, household air pollution and
  ozone.
- **Source:** Health Effects Institute (HEI) and IHME, *State of Global Air 2025*,
  India country profile. Data year 2023, published October 2025.
- **Link:** https://cdn.zevross.com/hei/country-reports/v3/pdf/India.pdf
  (profiles listed at https://www.stateofglobalair.org/resources/countryprofiles)
- *(read directly)*
- **Used in:** deck slide 2.

### BRICS: 2.955 million deaths from outdoor PM2.5, 59% of the world total

> **Pitch line:** Outdoor fine-particle pollution killed nearly 3 million people
> in the five original BRICS countries in 2023, 59% of all such deaths worldwide.

- **Quote:** "In 2023, APMP caused 2.955 million deaths in BRICS (59% of the
  global total)." APMP is ambient PM2.5 pollution. Original five members only.
- **Source:** Si et al., *BMJ Global Health* 2026;11(6):e023674, published June
  2026. Based on GBD 2023.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42331512/ (abstract read through NCBI
  E-utilities)
- *(read directly)*
- **Care:** outdoor PM2.5 only. Do not add it to, or set it beside, the HEI totals,
  which include household air pollution and ozone.
- **Used in:** deck slide 3.

## 2. The monitoring gap in India

### 47% of people beyond the grid, 12% of towns covered

> **Pitch line:** Nearly half of India's people live more than 50 km from any air
> quality station, and only 12% of its cities and towns have one.

- **Quotes:** "Of India's 4,041 census cities and towns, a mere 12 per cent have
  air quality monitoring systems"; "47 per cent population lives outside the air
  quality monitoring network"; "National monitoring grid is only 6-8 per cent of
  the recomemnded requirement" (the typo is in the original).
- **Source:** Centre for Science and Environment (CSE), analysis of CPCB station
  data to 31 December 2022, published 6 July 2023. "Outside the network" means
  beyond a 50 km radius of any station.
- **Link:** https://www.cseindia.org/only-12-per-cent-of-india-s-census-cities-and-towns-have-air-quality-monitoring-stations-11779
- *(read directly)*
- **Care:** an NGO's analysis of 2022 data. Say "CSE found", with the year.
- **Used in:** deck slide 2.

### 1,646 stations today against an estimated need of about 4,000

> **Pitch line:** India has 1,646 monitoring stations, manual and real-time
> together. One peer-reviewed estimate says it needs about 4,000.

- **Current network:** "As on March 2026, the ambient air-quality monitoring
  network in the country currently comprises 1,646 stations across 603 cities in
  28 States and 7 UTs, including manual stations and real-time stations."
  Press Information Bureau (PIB), explainer posted September 2026.
  https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?NoteId=159871&ModuleId=3&reg=24&lang=15
  *(read directly)*
- **Need:** "we estimate a need for 4000 stations in India: 2800 in the urban
  areas and 1200 in the rural areas." Brauer et al., *Atmospheric Environment*
  216 (2019) 116940. https://web.iitd.ac.in/~sagnik/AE2019c.pdf *(read directly)*
- **Care:** the two numbers come from different years and count stations
  differently; the 1,646 includes manual stations that sample twice a week. Say
  "one estimate", as the slide does.
- **Used in:** deck slide 2.

### Supporting, not on a slide

- **CPCB manual network:** "966 operating stations in 419 cities/towns … as on
  19.11.2024." https://cpcb.gov.in/about-namp/
- **Dey et al., *Remote Sensing* 2020 (CPCB co-authors):** "rural air quality
  monitoring from the surface does not exist in India"; satellite PM2.5 matched
  CPCB readings with R² of 0.8 (daily) and 0.97 (annual).
  https://cerca.iitd.ac.in/uploads/Research/1659512706A%20Satellite-Based%20High-Resolution%20(1-km)%20Ambient.pdf

## 3. Stubble smoke: a peak, not an average

### 37% on the worst day against 7.3% across the season

> **Pitch line:** Crop-burning smoke averages 7% of Delhi's PM2.5 over the
> season, but reached 37% on the worst day. Averages hide events.

  biomass-burning activities, and all other remaining regions on average

  contribution goes as high as 37%".
- **Source:** Govardhan et al., *Geoscientific Model Development* 17, 2617–2640,
  published April 2024. The team that runs the IITM Decision Support System.
  Data from the 2021–22 seasons.
- **Link:** https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/2617/2024/
- *(read directly)*

## 4. One sensor is not enough: burning moved after the satellite passes

### Over 90% of large fires after 3 pm; fire counts and burnt area disagree

> **Pitch line:** Farmers now burn after the midday satellite passes. Fire counts
> say burning fell by over 95%; burnt area says 25–35%.

- **Quotes:** "more than 90% of large farm fires in Punjab in 2024 and 2025
  occurred after 3:00 PM. In 2021, only 3% of large fires occurred after this
  time." And: "While burnt-area analysis shows clear reductions in farm fires in
  Punjab and Haryana (25–35%), these reductions are significantly lower than
  those suggested by active fire counts (>95%)."
- **Source:** iFOREST, *Stubble Burning Status Report 2025*, 8 December 2025.
  Uses Meteosat SEVIRI (every 15 minutes) and Sentinel-2 burnt-area mapping.
- **Link:** https://iforest.global/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/StubbleBurning_StatusReport_2025_Website.pdf
- *(read directly)*
- **Used in:** deck slide 3.

### NASA Earth Observatory confirms the shift

- **Quote:** "Most stubble fires now happen between 4 p.m. and 6 p.m. …
  fire-monitoring systems that rely solely on MODIS, or similar sensors like
  VIIRS…miss many of the fires."
- **Source:** NASA Earth Observatory, 15 December 2025.
- **Link:** https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/new-timing-for-stubble-burning-in-india
- *(read directly)*
- **Used in:** deck slide 3 (citation).

### Official fire counts, Punjab (context)

IARI CREAMS, 15 Sep–30 Nov each year: 83,002 (2020), 71,304 (2021), 49,922
(2022), 36,663 (2023), 10,909 (2024), 5,114 (2025). Bulletin 77, 30 Nov 2025:
https://creams.iari.res.in/Creams_website_bulletin/Fire_bulletin_2025/Fire_bulletin_2025r/77.RiceResidueFireBulletin_30Nov_2025_ICAR.pdf

Read these next to the iFOREST finding above: the drop in counts is partly a
drop in what the satellites can see.

## 6. South Africa: the Highveld and the Deadly Air case

### 12 Eskom coal power stations; a constitutional ruling upheld in 2025

> **Pitch line:** South Africa's Highveld holds 12 of Eskom's coal power
> stations. A court ruled its air breaches residents' constitutional rights, and
> the appeal court upheld that in 2025.

- **Quote (para 3):** "a 31 106 square km area … This area is an industrial home
  to 12 of Eskom's coal-fired power stations, Sasol's refinery and numerous coal
  mines."
- **The 2022 order, quoted in the appeal judgment:** "241.1 It is declared that
  the poor air quality in the Highveld Priority Area is in breach of residents'
  section 24(a) constitutional right to an environment that is not harmful to
  their health and well-being … 241.4 The Minister is directed, within 12 months
  of this order, to prepare, initiate, and prescribe regulations …"
- **Outcome:** the Supreme Court of Appeal dismissed the Minister's appeal but
  varied the order by "excising all the directions", including the one to improve
  the monitoring network.
- **Source:** Supreme Court of Appeal of South Africa, *Minister of Forestry,
  Fisheries and the Environment v groundWork Trust and Others*, 11 April 2025.
- **Link:** https://cer.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/DeadlyAir-SCA-11-April-2025.pdf
- *(read directly)*
- **Used in:** deck slide 3.

## Numbers we should NOT use

Each of these is easy to find and wrong, weak or misleading in the way we would
use it.

1. **"Stubble causes 40–48% of Delhi's pollution."** Those are single peak days
   (SAFAR, 2021). Season averages are 4–13%. If you use a peak, say "on the
   worst day" and give the year, as slide 2 does.
2. **"30% of Lahore's smog comes from India."** No primary source found. The
   World Bank's 30% runs the other way: from Pakistan's Punjab into India's.
3. **The CPCB series 13% / 11% / 10.6% / 3.5%.** From an RTI reply reported in
   The Tribune. We could not see the reply, and the averaging periods differ.
4. **"Fires fell 90%" read as "burning fell 90%".** Fire counts fell by over 95%;
   burnt area fell 25–35% (iFOREST). The gap is our argument, not our headline.
5. **"Satellites miss 90% of fires."** Headline wording. The finding is that over
   90% of *large* Punjab fires are lit after 3 pm.
6. **"7.2% of all deaths in India."** That study covers 10 cities and short-term
   exposure only.
7. **Mixing death totals.** 1.67M (2019), 2.1M (2021, SoGA 2024) and 2.01M
   (2023, SoGA 2025) come from different model runs. Use one, with its year.
   Likewise do not mix SoGA 2024 and SoGA 2025 figures for any country.
8. **Lahore "AQI 1,900".** A local reading reported in news; the US AQI scale
   stops at 500. The IQAir figure on record is 1,165 on 6 Nov 2024 (Al Jazeera).
9. **"1 monitor per 6.8 million people" and "4,000 monitors needed" without
   dates.** Both rest on 2010–2018 network data. The 4,000 is on slide 2 only as
   "one estimate", beside the current count.
10. **Airvoice's "40% of districts have no monitor" on its own.** A private
    company's analysis. Fine as support, not as a headline.
11. **Aakash project's 16% (2023) and 9% (2024) stubble shares.** Seen only in
    search snippets, not in the paper.
12. **AQLI "46% of Indians above the national standard".** Seen only on
    exam-prep sites.
13. **"2024 was Brazil's record fire year."** False. 2024 ranks seventh for
    Brazil (the peak is 2007, 393,915). For the Amazon, "most since 2007" is
    right.
14. **SoGA sector deaths** (for example South Africa's energy sector). They come
    from the older GBD-MAPS study, not 2023 data.
15. **"15.41 million" air pollution deaths in India in 2019** (PMID 38995431).
    Clearly wrong; probably confuses deaths with DALYs.
16. **Martin 2019's "141 countries with no monitoring".** Old data. Use OpenAQ
    2024's 71 countries.
17. **CREA's "79,500 deaths" from Eskom as a yearly figure.** It is a projected
    total from 2025 to the plants' end of life.
18. **Holland's 2,239 South African deaths a year (2017).** Commissioned by an
    NGO, not peer-reviewed.
19. **São Paulo "the world's most polluted city".** It had the worst air of about
    120 big cities in IQAir's live ranking for five days in September 2024 — not
    an annual title.
20. **Yakutia smoke "reached the North Pole".** News attributes it to NASA; the
    NASA page does not say it.
21. **"A court ordered better air monitoring" in the Deadly Air case.** The
    appeal court removed that direction in 2025.
22. **China's "1,436 stations" and "40.8% cleaner" beside the PNAS 61.8 to 42.0
    decline.** Different baselines. We did not find the 1,436 on a primary page.
23. **NASA Earth Observatory's "one monitor per 7 million people in India".**
    The page says only "by one estimate".
24. **CSE's 47% as anything other than an NGO analysis of 2022 data.**
25. **Roshydromet station counts for Russia.** They include occupied Ukrainian
    territory.
26. **"Stubble smoke reaches Delhi a day and a half later."** No source found;
    it was dropped from the deck.
